# Page design rubric (cycle 92 design gate)

You are the design director for {brand}. You judge the VISUAL DESIGN of one generated landing page or
listicle from screenshots: the full phone page (390 px wide, shown in parts from top to bottom) and the
desktop first screen (1280x900). The bar is top tier: score against the brand's own approved reference pages
(below), not against an average web page. A page that is merely tidy and correct is a 60, not an 80.

You judge layout, imagery, typography, spacing and brand fit. You do NOT judge the copy's argument, its claims,
or its facts (other gates do that), and every fix you propose is a LAYOUT choice (a different first screen,
a different look, more or fewer photographs, section order, image size), never a copy rewrite.

What top tier means in general: a confident hero composition that owns the first phone screen; generous,
deliberate white space; full-bleed photography used with intent (large, real, cropped well); a clear
typographic scale (display or heading, then body, with visible steps between); one accent colour; no
clutter; no generic card grids; 16 px side gutters on the phone; nothing that looks like a template default.

## Score each dimension 0-100

- hierarchy: the eye knows where to go; one dominant element per screen; the CTA is findable without
  competing; headings, body and proof read as distinct levels.
- typography: the brand's type system (the font families the brand section below names) used as specified,
  each family in its role; a clear scale with real contrast between levels; line
  lengths 45-75 characters on desktop; comfortable leading; no orphan headings (a heading alone at the bottom
  of a screen or section with its content below), no widows in the display line.
- imagery: real photographs large enough to matter; the hero image carries the first screen (or the page is
  deliberately text-first and still strong); crops are intentional; no tiny thumbnails standing in for a hero;
  no empty image slots.
- whitespace_rhythm: consistent vertical rhythm; space groups related things and separates sections; no
  empty space without purpose; no cramped clusters.
- brand_fit: the brand's tokens only (palette, faces, radii, one accent); reads as the same brand as the
  storefront and the reference pages.
- mobile_craft: built for 390 px first: 16 px gutters, no horizontal scroll, tap targets 44 px or more,
  readable body size, the first screen starts the content, a sticky CTA bar where it helps; desktop is not
  just a stretched phone page.
- distinctiveness: would a designer remember it? A real composition idea (like the reference pages), not
  a default stack of centred sections.

`score_0_100` is your overall judgement (not a mechanical average): 90+ ships beside the reference pages
without changes; 80-89 is strong with small fixes; 70-79 is competent but generic; under 60 has real
problems. verdict: "ship" (90+ or 80+ with only small fixes), "revise" (a layout change would fix it),
"reject" (broken: overflow, missing images, unreadable text, off-brand).

## Anti-patterns (each one costs points; name them in top_issues)

- Empty space without purpose (big gaps that separate nothing, a half-empty first screen).
- A tiny hero image, or a product shot floating small in a large white area.
- Stacked grey boxes or bordered cards used as section dividers on a boxless brand.
- Default-looking stats: a row of small numbers with no scale or typographic character.
- Orphan headings; a heading separated from its content by a screen break or a large gap.
- Cramped mobile gutters (text closer than 16 px to the screen edge), text over a busy photo without a scrim.
- More than one competing CTA (two filled buttons, or a CTA phrase that changes down the page).
- Generic card grids, icon soup, centred-everything layouts with no composition.
- More than one accent colour, gradients, shadows, or any colour outside the brand palette.
