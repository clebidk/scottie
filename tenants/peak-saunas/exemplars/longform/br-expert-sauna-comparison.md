## Full-Spectrum Infrared: The Wavelength Gap Most Brands Won't Discuss

Not all infrared is the same. This seems obvious when you say it out loud, but the marketing from most sauna brands is carefully crafted to obscure it. Here's the breakdown:
Far infrared (FIR) — the longest wavelengths, 5.6–1000 microns — penetrates the body at about 1–2 inches. It creates core heat, promotes sweating, and supports cardiovascular circulation. This is what most saunas deliver, and it's valuable. But it's incomplete.
Mid infrared (MIR) — penetrates deeper into soft tissue and muscle, reaching 3–4 inches. Research suggests it enhances circulation, reduces muscle pain, and amplifies the cardiovascular effects of heat therapy. Most saunas don't include it.
Near infrared (NIR) — the shortest wavelengths, penetrating deepest into cellular tissue and even the brain. NIR stimulates mitochondrial activity through photobiomodulation, meaning it triggers cellular energy production at a biological level. The research here is particularly compelling for cognitive health, cellular repair, and anti-aging pathways.
"A far-infrared-only sauna is like a multivitamin that only contains Vitamin C. It's not bad. It's just not complete."
