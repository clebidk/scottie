### What the research actually shows

Regular sauna bathing is one of the most-studied longevity habits in the world. The strongest evidence comes from a 20-year Finnish cohort study that followed 2,315 middle-aged men and compared those who bathed 4–7 times a week against once-a-week users.
- Longevity. 40% lower all-cause mortality among the most frequent users (Laukkanen et al., JAMA Internal Medicine, 2015).
- Cardiovascular. 50% lower fatal cardiovascular disease in the same group (same study); a later analysis also linked frequent sauna use to lower incidence of hypertension (American Journal of Hypertension, 2017).
- Brain health. 65% lower rate of Alzheimer’s disease and dementia among the most frequent users (Age and Ageing, 2017).
- Recovery and relaxation. Heat exposure raises circulation and is widely used for post-exercise recovery, muscle relaxation, and winding down at the end of a day.
Two things worth knowing about this research. It studied sauna bathing generally — largely traditional Finnish saunas — so it is evidence about the habit, not about any one brand. And the benefits tracked with frequency: 4–7 sessions a week, not the occasional visit. Consistency at that level is difficult to buy by the session, which is the practical case for owning one.

There is a genuine body of peer-reviewed research, and it is worth being precise about what it says. The most-cited work followed 2,315 middle-aged Finnish men for two decades and found that frequent sauna bathers (4–7x/week) had 40% lower all-cause mortality and 50% lower fatal cardiovascular disease than once-a-week bathers (JAMA Internal Medicine, 2015), with a follow-up reporting a 65% lower rate of Alzheimer’s and dementia (Age and Ageing, 2017). Those studies looked at sauna bathing as a habit — mostly traditional Finnish saunas — not at any particular brand or at infrared specifically.
